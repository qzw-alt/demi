# Outdoor-Room Walk Thread Pattern (verified 2026-07-01 → 2026-07-04)

## The pattern

When the monthly content calendar calls for "Outdoor Feng Shui" / "Garden Tips" / any property-wide outdoor theme, walk the outdoor rooms in **order of homeowner control** (most → least). This gives each article a natural reason to exist and a coherent arc.

## The "Fu Wei ladder" framing

Each article uses the same move set, scaled to the homeowner's actual control over that room:

| Move | Front Garden | Back Garden | Balcony | Rooftop |
|------|--------------|-------------|---------|---------|
| 1. Clear what's not yours | Clear the path of obstacles | Clear the compost corner | Take everything off the floor that isn't furniture | Check what you didn't put there (building equipment, abandoned objects) |
| 2. Look from inside | Look from the front door | Look from the back door | Look from the kitchen/living room | Look from the access door |
| 3. Finish the far corner | Finish the corner furthest from the gate | Finish the corner furthest from the back door | Finish the corner furthest from the balcony door | Finish the corner furthest from the access door |
| 4. Add one element | One Water feature | One Metal anchor | One Metal anchor | One Metal anchor |
| 5. Hang nothing on the outside | No signage over the gate | No climbing plants over the back fence | Nothing hanging from outside the railing | Nothing hanging from outside the parapet |

The repetition of the move set across articles is intentional — the reader who lands on any one article gets the full recommendation; the reader who reads the series sees the moves tighten as homeowner control decreases.

## Lead-sentence formula

Each outdoor-room article's lead paragraph references the prior article with one of these patterns:

- "Yesterday walked the [previous room]. Today walks [this room]."
- "Yesterday walked the smallest outdoor space. Today climbs to the highest one."
- "The July thread has been walking outdoor spaces in order of how much the homeowner can change them."

The first pattern (06-30 / 07-01 → 07-04) is the canonical form — it both re-anchors the prior article and previews the move set for the current one.

## Classical term for the rooftop: sky Fu Wei

The classical practitioner-level term for the rooftop in a four-outdoor-room reading is the **sky Fu Wei**. The seasonal qi is read as rising from the ground, settling on the roof, and moving downward through the building. A rooftop that reads cluttered reads cluttered all the way to the lobby. **This framing is the differentiator from a generic "rooftop decoration" article** — the cycles read the rooftop before they read any other outdoor space, and the homeowner-level articles almost always skip this hierarchy.

## Article skeleton (each outdoor-room piece should include)

1. **"Why this room gets today's article" H2** — 2-3 paragraphs explaining why this room is the next step in the thread, what Fu Wei type it is, and what the Earth Month specifically asks of it
2. **"What to do this week, in the order [room] asks" H2** — 5-6 numbered moves in execution order, each starting with a bold verb (Clear, Look, Finish, Add, Hang, Place)
3. **"What [room] is asking for in early July" H2** — 2-3 paragraphs + an H3 subsection on the room-specific boundary question (gate, fence, railing, parapet)
4. **"Day-master-by-element reading for [room]" H2** — 5 paragraphs, one per element (Wood/Fire/Earth/Metal/Water), with bold element names and the chart-side interpretation
5. **"What to undo at Li Qiu" H2** — 3 specific undos (late-Fire lighting, the "this is optional" assumption, summer-specific temporary structures)
6. **7 FAQs** covering room-specific edge cases — for the rooftop: shared building, planter bans, solar panels, view as element, mechanical equipment, floor material, new access
7. **CTA section** — same Day Master pitch across all articles, links to `instant_reading.html`
8. **Footer cross-links** — to the prior 2-3 outdoor-room articles, plus Five Elements Explained + Bagua Map Guide + Home

## Recommended article length
