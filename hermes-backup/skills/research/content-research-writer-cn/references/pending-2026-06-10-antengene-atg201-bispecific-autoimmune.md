## Key facts (verified from the press release — DO NOT re-derive)

- **Company:** Antengene Corporation Limited, SEHK:6996.HK, headquartered in Shanghai with operations in Hong Kong
- **Asset:** ATG-201 — CD19 × CD3 bispecific T-cell engager (TCE) with **steric hindrance masking** technology (designed to limit off-target activity / on-target off-tumor toxicity by keeping the CD3-binding arm masked until CD19 engagement pulls it into proximity with a B cell)
- **Mechanism:** redirects autologous T cells to CD19-expressing B cells, eliminating pathogenic B cells
- **Indication:** B-cell related autoimmune diseases (umbrella — concrete indications to name in the article include refractory systemic lupus erythematosus, rheumatoid arthritis, ANCA-associated vasculitis, and immune thrombocytopenia; the trial itself is indication-agnostic at Phase I)
- **Trial:** Phase I ATTRACT study — dose escalation + dose expansion; primary objectives safety/tolerability + RP2D determination; secondary objectives PK/PD, immunogenicity, preliminary efficacy
- **Geography:** Phase I in China (Antengene runs) + Australia (Antengene runs); then UCB takes over worldwide development
- **Principal investigator:** Prof. Zhanguo Li (栗占国), Peking University People's Hospital — a senior Chinese rheumatologist and longtime autoimmune-disease KOL
- **License deal:** Antengene + UCB (Belgian pharma, major in autoimmune/neuro: Cimzia certolizumab, Rystiggo rozanolixizumab, Zilbrysq) — UCB has worldwide exclusive license to develop, manufacture, and commercialize ATG-201 plus access to manufacturing technology. Antengene retains Phase I rights and conducts the FIH trials in China and Australia, then transfers everything to UCB.
- **Status:** NMPA IND cleared; Phase I not yet dosed (no first-patient-in date in the press release)

## Why it matters for medical tourism (the framing for the article)

1. **Beijing autoimmunity is a real inbound category.** Peking University People's Hospital (PKUPH) rheumatology is one of the top Chinese centers for refractory autoimmune disease. International patients with refractory lupus, vasculitis, or immune cytopenias who have failed standard-of-care (steroids + cyclophosphamide / MMF / belimumab / rituximab) are a real inbound flow to Beijing. PKUPH has an international patient office.
2. **Bispecifics are expensive in the West.** Blinatumomab (CD19/CD3, Amgen) for B-ALL runs ~$178K per course in the US. Tecvayli (BCMA/CD3) for myeloma runs ~$270K+ first year. CD19-directed bispecifics for autoimmune disease are at the cusp of US/EU launch (Sanofi frexalimab in MS; several anti-CD20/BCMA bispecifics in lupus trials). A Chinese-bisimilar access route — even at trial pricing — is meaningful for self-pay international patients who cannot access these drugs at home due to non-approval or cost.
3. **UCB validation is the regulatory anchor.** A Phase III-ready pipeline asset licensed to UCB carries an implicit FDA/EMA regulatory pathway. Mentioning the UCB deal in the article is a credibility marker.
4. **Steric hindrance masking is the technical hook.** Most lay readers (and many clinicians) don't realize that bispecific T-cell engagers can be engineered with a "mask" that prevents the T-cell arm from engaging CD3 until the antibody binds the tumor target. This is what makes CD19 TCEs feasible in autoimmune disease — without the mask, a CD19 TCE would deplete B cells indiscriminately, including healthy B cells, but the mask puts a brake on off-target activity. ATG-201 has this. Blinatumomab does not (continuous infusion is used to manage CRS).
